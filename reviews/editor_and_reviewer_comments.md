**Reviewer 1**

The authors have developed a metadata resource, the Human Respiratory
Viral Immunization Longitudinal AI Gene Expression atlas
(HR-VILAGE-3K3M), integrating bulk and single-cell transcriptomic
profiles from 3,178 subjects across 66 studies, spanning vaccination,
inoculation, and mixed-exposure contexts, with samples from blood and
nasal swabs aggregated from GEO, ImmPort, and ArrayExpress. While this
could be a valuable resource for the vaccine research community, the
resource is not comprehensive and appears to have missed several key
datasets, and address the key issues prior to the publication.\
\
Major comments:\
1. Authors should provide PRISMA workflow describing data collection
process.\
2. Most of these vaccine studies are done on adults \<60 years. Authors
should explicitly show how many samples are coming from the older adults
(Age distribution of the sample for longitudinal time points). - For
both scRNASeq, bulk RNAseq and microaary\
3. What is sample ratio based on vaccine type (Flu IAV, LIAV, Fluzone
HS, FluAd, Flublok, FLuarix, similarly COVID vaccines and others)\
4. Define Inoculation and mixed exposure.\
5. How are single cell data dealt? How are cell types defined- clarify
on those details\
6. Donors with high baseline titers mask seroconversion rates. How are
these donors dealt. Authors should consider using baseline adjusted
methods for response categorization (maxRBA from Steve Kleinstein's
group).\
7. How are they defining response status for other vaccines ?\
8. Authors should validate the overall processing data by checking know
vaccination read outs interferon signature at day1, plasma blast
expansion at day7 for influenza vaccine- Authors should consider
checking the geneses at these time points in case of transcriptome cell
types.

**Reviewer 2**

Summary:\
The paper presents a comprehensive respiratory viral transcriptomics
dataset HR-VILAGE-3K3M that integrates data from multiple platforms
(microarray, bulk RNAseq, and single-cell RNA-seq/scRNAseq). The data is
compiled from 66 independent studies covering 3000+ samples, with these
studies capturing measurements at different timepoints following
vaccination, experimental inoculation, and mixed exposures, using both
blood and nasal swab specimens. Demonstrated applications
(age/sex/antibody-responder prediction and celltype annotation), and
potential applications (gene expression imputation, cellular
deconvolution, etc.) are provided to highlight the utility of this
dataset. \
\
\
Strengths:\
+ The dataset has been curated and harmonized from 66 independent
studies, using various efforts such as resolving inconsistency in gene
and sample identifiers, obtaining antibody response data through
targeted email outreach to authors, and other data cleaning operations
(such as duplicate sample removal, completing the missing RNAseq
(pre)processing steps on certain studies, etc.). \
+ It has been made publicly available through Hugging Face, enabling
researchers to readily query the resource and perform downstream
analyses.\
+ The utility of the resource\'s single-platform data is demonstrated
using different bulk RNAseq-based prediction tasks, and that of the
multi-platform data is demonstrated using a cell-type annotation
application (via the integration of paired bulk and sc
RNAseq datasets). \
\
\
Major comments/concerns:\
There are multiple concerns that detract from the strengths of the
paper.  \
\
1) (compare with ImmPort) Explicit positioning of this resource amidst
related resources like ImmPort of HIPC (and associated visualizer
ImmuneSpace) should be mentioned in Introduction. Without this explicit
positioning, a reader would find it hard to assess the extra value added
by this resource atop existing resources like ImmPort. \
For instance, ImmPort had compiled 300 studies based on its 2018
publication in Scientific Data. The current work takes 66 studies,
probably many of them from ImmPort, relevant to respiratory viral
immunization (including both influenza and COVID) and harmonizes them.
Within this context, answering the questions below directly in the paper
can clarify the value addition of the HR-VILAGE resource:  \
- What fraction of studies in HR-VILAGE are taken from ImmPort? \
- Of the studies taken from ImmPort, which harmonization or data
cleaning was missing in ImmPort, and what curation/analysis has been
additionally done in HR-VILAGE-3K3M to address these harmonization
issues? For instance, it is hard to believe that ImmPort did not already
handle gene ID issues like SEPTIN14.\
- For the studies in HR-VILAGE taken from GEO and ArrayExpress, was more
effort required to perform data cleaning and harmonization (compared to
studies taken from ImmPort)? \
\
2a) (other modalities) A key limitation of HR-VILAGE-3K3M compared to
ImmPort is its restriction to transcriptomic/antibody-response data and
ImmPort\'s additional support for flow cytometry, cytokine, CyTOF, and
related datasets. What is the rationale for this restriction, given that
systems immunology research has largely advanced in recent times due to
integrative analysis of multi-modal (transcriptomic, flow cytometry,
cytokine, antibody response, etc.) data collected from immune-related
human cohorts? Won\'t this restriction limit the utility of HR-VILAGE
for future systems immunology analyses or re-analyses? This limitation
either needs to be acknowledged, alongside the advantages of
HR-VILAGE over ImmPort; or addressed by including analysis-ready data
pertaining to some of these extra modalities in HR-VILAGE. 

2b) A related point on missing modalities is different assays of
immune/antibody response such as MN (microneutralization) titers, HAI,
and ELISPOT. There is mention of HAI in the paper, but the paper is not
clear about whether other assays such as MN and ELISPOT are stored in
HR-VILAGE. Are there studies that have jointly assayed these different
immune endpoints? If so, it would be useful to store these different
endpoints in HR-VILAGE to enable alternative definitions of
antibody-responders (including continuous-valued response status, as
well as binary responder vs. non-responder status) to compare/contrast
these outcome definitions from biological as well as statistical
viewpoints (e.g., fold change of antibody titers that is adjusted for
its inverse relation to initial/baseline titers is a biologically
relevant endpoint - can HR-VILAGE enable prediction of such adjusted FC
outcomes?; also can the resource help answer which outcome definitions
are more or less predictable using statistical/ML/DL approaches).\
\
3) (compare with other repo) Comparative analysis of this resource with
other independent state-of-the-art resources is missing. While all
studies in the HR-VILAGE resource may not be available in other
independent resources, certain studies in HR-VILAGE may also be present
in other transcriptomic resources such as recounts{,2} and Gene
Expression Commons (GEXC). For such common studies, what is the
qualitative difference between the data preprocessing pipelines employed
by the different resources, and can the authors importantly quantify the
similarities and differences in these resources\' processed datasets and
downstream results? This comparison would help the readers to increase
their trust on the processed datasets in HR-VILAGE and convince them to
use it. \
\
4a) (why QN, explain AUC) What was the rationale for selecting quantile
normalization (QN) over alternative approaches for RNAseq harmonization?
Was QN performed in a class-specific manner (i.e., stratified by
responder group as high, medium, low) rather than across the pooled
dataset? Prior work has shown that whole-dataset QN can distort true
biological differences, particularly when class-effect proportions are
high, whereas class-specific QN better preserves such differences (Zhao,
Wong & Goh, Sci Rep, 2020: 
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-72664-6). \
4b) On a related point, could the authors clarify whether the
Transformer model used for antibody responder prediction was trained
directly on QN-normalized gene expression values? If so, how much of the
Transformer\'s predictive performance (AUC = 0.737) can be attributed to
genuine biological signal vs. artifacts of the QN-induced rank-based
rescaling?\
\
5a) (study specific pipline) While standardized/uniform (pre)processing
pipelines employed by HR-VILAGE (such as using microarray preprocessing
and (class-specific) QN steps) is preferable to reduce methodological
variations in the resulting datasets, another viable strategy is to
preprocess datasets from different studies in a highly study-specific
manner to account for study-specific artifacts. The authors of a
particular study may be best equipped to choose appropriate
preprocessing (including transformation and normalization) methods to
apply on their dataset, since they are the ones most knowledgeable about
the underlying experimental design, batch effects, and all relevant
metadata (including those not captured online). Given this context, it
is highly advisable for HR-VILAGE to not only provide the standardized
analysis-ready data for a study, but also the analysis-ready data
provided by the study\'s authors whenever available (e.g., from
websites/github linked to papers such as illustrated here
https://www.tsanglab.org/resources).\
5b) Conversely, focusing on RNAseq data, gene counts data provided by
study authors is used for certain studies in HR-VILAGE, and gene counts
from standardized analysis (alignment of fastq files and counts
summarization) is used for other studies in HR-VILAGE.  For each study,
both study-author-provided and uniformly-processed pipeline based
versions of the gene counts matrix should be provided. That is, even
when RNAseq based gene counts matrix is available for certain studies,
HR-VILAGE should also provide gene counts recomputed from its standard
pipeline of aligning RNAseq reads and summarizing gene counts. \
The outcome of addressing the 5a and 5b comments above is two versions
of HR-VILAGE resource: HR-VILAGE-study_specific_processing and
HR-VILAGE-uniform_processing. These two versions are useful, since they
can now be compared as a QC step (e.g., using tSNE/PCA plots as in Fig.
2a) to flag samples with discrepancies between the two analysis
pipelines (study authors\' vs. HR-VILAGE \'s uniform pipeline).\
\
6) (compare application results with publications) For any of the three
applications showed, please compare your results with previously
published results to indicate what is common, and more importantly also,
what is different, so that there is motivation to do reanalysis of these
studies, or reinterpret one study in the context of other studies (as in
a meta-analysis) to get novel insights. I understand that this is a
dataset-focused journal and the applications are there to only support
the curated dataset, but presenting these conserved vs. novel findings
using simple venn diagram overlaps of marker genes predictive of certain
outcomes say, may even suffice to encourage researchers to use this
resource for reanalyzing existing viral transcriptomic datasets in
HR-VILAGE.\
